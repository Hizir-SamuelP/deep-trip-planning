# Budget, Payment, and Customs

## 1. Build a checkable budget

Keep two blocks before totaling: home-currency commitments paid before departure (flights, visas, insurance, deposits) and destination-currency spending (lodging, food, local and long-distance transport, tickets, incidentals). State the exchange assumption and what a ±5% movement changes. Break categories down enough to test them: “lunch X, dinner Y, convenience-store breakfast Z for eight days,” not “food 3,500.”

## 2. Find the uncapped category

Most costs have a natural ceiling. Shopping does not; for another traveler it may be gambling, spa, fine dining, or performances. Give it restrained, normal, and unrestricted bands, say what each buys, add each to the total, and ask the traveler to record a hard cap on their phone. Without one, the category quietly destroys the usefulness of the rest of the budget.

## 3. Make the adjustable switches visible

After the table, label: costs that can be cut, with a specific amount; costs not worth cutting, with what time or certainty they buy; and costs that make the total unstable, with sensitivity. This turns a budget from one opaque number into levers the traveler can actually move.

## 4. Customs and tax refund

Use official border sources to check personal-use allowance, how excess is taxed (including whole-item taxation where applicable), separate alcohol/tobacco limits, and the departure country's refund process. Convert each shopping band into the entry country's currency and explain when it crosses the allowance. Tax refunds change the departure-day route: leave 30–45 minutes when the official process requires it and keep possible inspection items accessible rather than buried in checked baggage.

## 5. Cash and payment

Do not assume cards or mobile payment work everywhere. State a sourced cash amount, likely cash-only contexts, locally accepted payment methods, withdrawal fees, transit-card top-up limits for foreign cards, and the fact that departure refunds can delay cash recovery. Prepare the conservative payment path until the local rule is verified.

## 6. Baggage allowance

Shopping-heavy trips need baggage math before departure. Leave roughly half of a suitcase empty when appropriate, check airline piece/weight rules, identify heavy purchases, buy extra allowance online rather than at the airport, and verify liquids, power banks, and lithium batteries with the airline.

## 7. Insurance and emergency

Reserve budget for insurance and state what it protects; an overseas emergency can exceed the premium many times over. Put policy number and assistance line, local emergency/police numbers, consular assistance, disaster-alert apps, and trip-specific medicines in the plan. Verify all changing limits and rules for this trip, not from a blog or memory.
