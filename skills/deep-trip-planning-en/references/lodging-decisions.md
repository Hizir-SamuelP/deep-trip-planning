# Lodging Decisions

Lodging is the trip's foundation: it sets commuting time, laundry options, and how forgiving arrival and departure days are. It also invites endless browsing, so this guide is about closing the decision.

## 1. Compare all-in totals only

Headline prices are incomparable across tax treatment, cleaning/city/service fees, logged-in discounts, currencies, exchange rates, and cancellation terms. Convert every candidate into the all-fee total for N nights in one currency. Include total, room size/bed type, cancellation deadline, rating and review count, and location. Until that conversion is done, “A is cheaper than B” is not a valid conclusion.

## 2. Read ratings with their sample size

An 8.0 from 700 reviews and a 7.9 from 200 are not equally certain. Treat a high score under 100 reviews cautiously. Read component ratings as well as the average: location, cleanliness, comfort, and value expose the weakness. Facilities and cleanliness matter more on longer stays; location matters more on short stays.

## 3. Amenity-listing traps

Platform checkboxes are evidence when present, not proof of absence when omitted. A washer is not a dryer. A kitchenette may mean a sink and hot plate, not an oven or usable cookware. “Upper floors accessible by stairs only” effectively means no elevator, which matters with luggage. Twin beds can be adjacent Hollywood twins, not separated beds; inspect room photos. A room type assigned on arrival does not guarantee bed type and should be rejected when beds are a hard requirement.

Verify the traveler's must-haves—bed configuration, laundry, elevator, and size—against both the amenity table and guest comments. Put unresolved items in a pre-departure question list.

## 4. Judge location against the itinerary

“Near downtown” is empty. From the origin station, calculate outbound and return time for each high-frequency area, then count which days the option wins or loses. A saving on the earliest morning, luggage day, or district visited three times is worth more than ten minutes on a one-off ordinary day. Use the actual address and nearest station, not the neighborhood in the property name.

## 5. The non-refundable threshold

Non-refundable rates buy a lower price by removing adjustment room for weather, flight changes, or health. Agree a discount threshold first and enforce it. Ten to fifteen percent can start the discussion, not end it: a fragile trip may need far more, while a fixed trip may accept less. The order matters—set the threshold before seeing the price, or every price will acquire a rationalization.

## 6. Close the decision

Close it when one condition is met: the current choice is best in its all-in class and no rival clears the threshold; the free-cancellation deadline removes the practical window; or only the current option satisfies a hard need. Then write “do not compare further,” put the cancellation deadline in the countdown, and state a precise B-plan trigger, such as a host failing to provide clear access instructions before the deadline. Decision closure is part of the deliverable.

## 7. Self-check-in and apartment risk

For self-service lodging, make a 48-hour confirmation list: lockbox code and steps, building number, emergency contact and language, floor/elevator, pre- and post-checkout luggage storage, and quiet-hour rules. Any unclear answer before the free-cancellation deadline is a B-plan trigger, not a question to leave until departure day.
