# Verification Method

This is the step most likely to be faked and the one most likely to prevent a bad day on the ground:

> “This place exists and is open” is not the same as “it is open on my planned date, at my planned hour.”

“Open now” on a map describes the present moment, not a Tuesday three months from now.

## 1. Minimum check for every venue

For every place the traveler intends to enter, check all three:

1. **What weekday is the planned date?** Put it in the day heading. Many venues close every Wednesday or on the second and fourth Tuesday.
2. **Is it open that weekday?** Check the official page and temporary-closure notices.
3. **Does the planned arrival fall inside the real opening window?** Account for midday breaks, last order (often 30–60 minutes before closing), and late opening.

Then reverse-check the route. If the first stop opens at noon, a 09:00 departure creates two hours of dead time; reorder the day or choose an early-opening stop. For a shop inside a mall, verify the mall's hours as well as the shop's.

## 2. Calendar traps

Check every trip date for public holidays, especially runs of three or more non-working days. Long weekends raise crowds at stations, attractions, restaurants, lockers, airports, and long-distance transport; they can also change opening hours and prices. Revisit whether the most time-sensitive day—often arrival or departure—lands on the first day of that run.

Also check for rules that take effect during the trip: tax refund, visas, entry registration, or ticketing systems. Search for local festivals, anniversaries, major conventions, school holidays, and events near the specific venues, not merely national holidays.

## 3. Seasonal expectations

“That season” is not a forecast. Find the **current prediction window for the exact location**, then compare it against each travel date.

For example, a five-day trip runs April 10–14 and the official blossom window is April 7–10:

- ❌ “You will catch blossom season.” Four of the five days are already past peak.
- ❌ “You will miss it.” Arrival day still overlaps.
- ✅ “Only arrival day, April 10, is the last peak day. Go that afternoon; do not leave it for later. From April 12, treat the city as post-peak and plan for fallen petals or other sights.”

One overlapping day is the only opportunity and belongs at the front of the itinerary. Label the outcome with dates: on time, marginal (state when it starts declining and the substitute), or missed (say not to make a special trip). Altitude and latitude can shift the same country's season by weeks. Tell the traveler how to recheck three days before departure using an official attraction, park, weather, or forecast source.

## 4. Golden hours and back-solving

For sunrise, sunset, uncrowded opening, favorable light, tides, or timed illumination: find the desired arrival time and why it matters; work backward through local transit, long-distance transit, departure, and wake-up; and change transport before accepting a late arrival. An obscure earlier bus may beat a famous express if it is the only service that reaches the required time.

## 5. Use social sources for the right things

Personal trip reports reveal things official pages omit: the quietest time, correct exit, ticket-window quirks, bus queues, or lockers that fill at noon. Prefer recent firsthand posts and their comments; filter out agencies, commercial promotion, and AI summaries. Use social evidence for experience and traps, then verify hours, prices, and reservation rules with official sources. Never buy booking, group-buy, or guiding services through social media.

## 6. Write verification into the plan

Record the verification state, not just the answer: link officially checked pages and their date checked; label social-only leads and their date; for gaps, write “recheck X days before departure” and exactly where. Hard facts—hours, closure days, prices, fares, transfer counts, reservation rules, holidays, live seasonal windows, and step and slope conditions—need a source from this planning session. Training-data memories are stale. End with sources, then repeat critical checks seven days before departure and on departure day.
