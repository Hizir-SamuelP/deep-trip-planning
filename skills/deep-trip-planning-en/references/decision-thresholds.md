# Decision Thresholds and Source Boundaries

These rules turn “we will see later” into “do this when that condition occurs.” Every number comes from the traveler's preferences in step 0.5; examples are formats, not recommended defaults.

**Queue limit.** Give each venue a walk-away rule and named alternative: “after X minutes, go to Y.” The wrong threshold makes the plan controlling; no threshold lets one queue consume a day.

**Candidates are not a checklist.** Offer several where uncertainty warrants it, but say the traveler chooses one. A flexible traveler may need three to five; a certainty-first traveler may need one primary and one backup.

**Meal density and reservations.** Ask whether food is the trip's main event or fuel. Reservations can be valuable, but every reservation anchors half a day of routing. Only sellout or required-seat cases are objectively mandatory; the rest follows the user's certainty preference.

**Back-solving.** For sunrise, opening, light, or tide, work back from the required arrival. If it fails, change transport rather than accepting a weaker result.

**Paying for time.** When an uncertain leg threatens the day's only irreplaceable window, a taxi, private car, or reserved seat can be the best-value expense. List the cost separately and say what certainty it buys.

**A/B switch threshold.** Decide what B must improve over A before comparing prices. If it does not clear that bar, write “do not compare further.” Repeated comparison is itself a cost.

## What each source is for

| Source | Use it for | Do not use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Personal social posts | Route feel, food ideas, practical traps, lived experience | Hours, prices, reservation rules |
| Official venue pages | Hours, closure days, prices, booking, rules | Crowd feel or taste |
| Ticket/lodging platforms | Live inventory, all-in totals, cancellation terms | Whether a location is genuinely convenient |
| Maps | Distance, route geometry, exits | Future operating status |

Social posts reveal details official sources omit—quiet times, correct exits, sold-out windows, bus queues, full lockers—but they remain leads. Recheck hard facts with official sources. Never buy booking, group-buy, or guiding services through social media.
