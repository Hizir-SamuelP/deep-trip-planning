# Transit and Maps

Start every day from a fixed origin. For public transit, write line, boarding station, transfer station, destination, duration, fare, and a relevant trap: express trains that skip a stop, physically separate same-named stations, last departures, exit choice, or long internal walks. For driving, write route, drive time, parking, tolls, fuel, road restrictions, and offline-navigation coverage.

Use official operators for schedules, fares, closures, and ticket rules. Maps establish distance, walking geometry, and exits; they are not reliable proof of future operating status. Compare passes against individual fares with a visible calculation; default to pay-as-you-go when a pass does not clearly win.

Maintain three map layers: a planning map for grouping, a navigable map for the day, and offline copies/addresses for failure conditions. A custom map is neither offline nor turn-by-turn navigation.
